Lessons

Why digits cannot be predicted

Synthetic indices use a certified random number generator. Every tick is a fresh draw, so past digits carry no information about the next one. Charts of "hot" and "cold" digits describe history, not the future.

The gambler's fallacy

After five evens in a row, the next tick is still 50% even. Streaks feel meaningful because people notice them, but they appear naturally in random sequences. Use the pattern section above to see how common long runs are.

Break-even and the house edge

A 95% payout on a 50% contract means you win 0.95 when right and lose 1.00 when wrong. You need about 51.3% wins to break even, but the true chance is 50%, so the expected result is negative on every trade. Try it in the calculator.

Matches/Differs and Over/Under

Differs wins 90% of the time but pays little, so one loss wipes out many wins. Matches wins 10% and pays a lot. Both are priced to give the platform the same edge. Always check the true probability against the payout.

Protecting your money

Only trade what you can afford to lose. Set a daily loss limit before you start and stop when you hit it. Do not raise stakes to win back losses. Use the journal to check your real record rather than your memory, and be wary of anyone selling signals or guaranteed win rates.
